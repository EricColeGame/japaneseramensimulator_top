## What the Steam Store Actually Confirms

The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4968640/) lists Wild Dog as the developer with a release date of 9 October 2026. According to the store description, the game is a ramen shop management simulator for up to six players. The advertised activity list is specific:

## How the Work Splits in a Six-Player Kitchen

The advertised task list maps naturally onto roles, and this is the most practical way to think about the game before you have played it. In a six-player session, the obvious division is ingredient prep, cooking, serving, customer care, and cleaning, with one person floating to wherever the queue is backing up.

The value of that structure is that no single player has to be a superhero. Ramen is a fast, timing-sensitive product: broth and noodles have narrow windows, toppings are assembled at the end, and a bowl that sits too long is a bowl that disappoints. Splitting prep from finishing lets the cook focus on timing while someone else handles the front of house.

This is general cooking and menu logic, drawn from how ramen shops actually operate. It is player practice, not a documented in-game system. Whether the game models noodle doneness, broth temperature, or topping freshness at that level of detail is something you can only confirm by playing or by reading the official store description.

## Designing a Menu You Can Actually Execute

The store page confirms you create your own menu, but it does not publish a recipe list or ingredient quantities. That means menu design guidance has to stay at the level of principle:

1. **Start narrow.** A menu with two or three bowls you can execute cleanly beats a long menu that stalls your kitchen. Fewer items usually means fewer distinct ingredients to prep and fewer ways for a six-player team to collide.
2. **Share base components.** If several bowls use the same broth or the same tare, prep work overlaps and your team spends less time on duplicate tasks. This is standard kitchen economy, not a leaked recipe.
3. **Add complexity after you are consistent.** Once the team can serve a simple menu without a backlog, expand. Adding a signature bowl is a reward, not a starting condition.
4. **Match the menu to your shop layout.** Since you also design the shop, treat the two decisions as one. A menu that sends everyone to the same corner of the kitchen will bottleneck no matter how good the recipe is.

If the game does expose numeric recipe data in the menu builder, that is exactly where you will find it, inside the game itself rather than on a wiki written from memory.

## Choosing First-Person or Third-Person View

The store description says players can switch between first-person and third-person views, and can choose a view if prone to motion sickness. This is one of the few pieces of genuinely useful pre-purchase information on the page.

First-person tends to suit players who want immersion and precise station interaction. Third-person tends to suit players who want spatial awareness of the kitchen and their teammates. In a co-op kitchen where people are moving around you constantly, that awareness is not a minor consideration. If you are unsure, test both early in a session rather than committing to one for hours.
