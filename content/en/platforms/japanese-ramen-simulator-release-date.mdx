## What the official store page says the game is

The Steam description is refreshingly concrete about the loop. Japanese Ramen Simulator is a ramen shop management simulator for up to six players. The advertised tasks cover the whole shift: preparing ingredients, cooking, serving food, looking after customers, and cleaning up. That list matters because it tells you the game treats the front of house and the back of house as one continuous job rather than splitting them into separate minigames.

Two features get their own emphasis on the store page. First, players create their own menu, which suggests the recipe system is authored by the players rather than handed to them as a fixed tech tree. Second, players design the shop, so layout and presentation are part of the project, not just a static set. The page also confirms you can switch between first-person and third-person views, and that the game lets you choose a view if you are prone to motion sickness. That last point is a small but genuinely useful accessibility note, and it is stated by the developer rather than inferred.

One thing the page does not do is publish a recipe list with exact ingredient quantities, prices, or any kind of code system. If you have seen screenshots that look like a recipe spreadsheet, treat that as speculation until you confirm it in-game.

Six players is a large crew for a kitchen, and the official feature list is broad enough to absorb that number. Preparing ingredients, cooking, serving, and cleaning are separate activities, so a group can split roles naturally: one or two people prepping, one on the broth and noodles, one plating and running food, one handling customers, and one keeping the shop from turning into a disaster zone. None of that division is dictated by the developer in the store text; it is simply what the advertised task list makes possible.

For menu logic, the safest practical approach is the one real kitchens use, and this is player practice rather than anything the game documents:

| Principle | Why it helps in a co-op kitchen |
| --- | --- |
| Keep the menu short at first | Fewer ingredients to prep, fewer ways for a six-person crew to collide |
| Build around one base broth | Shared prep means one player can own that station |
| Separate hot and cold tasks | Lets two people work without blocking each other's station |
| Add items only when the crew is bored, not overwhelmed | Menu growth should follow the team's speed, not the other way around |

No exact quantities, cook times, or prices are listed here, because none are documented. You will have to discover those by playing.
