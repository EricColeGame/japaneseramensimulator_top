## How a co-op shift tends to be structured

Based on the advertised loop, a session logically breaks into stages. This is reasoning from the official description, not a quoted developer breakdown.

**Prep stage.** Ingredients get prepared before service. In a six-player group, this is where splitting tasks matters most, because prep is usually the bottleneck that determines how smoothly service runs.

**Cooking stage.** Food gets cooked to order. Timing and coordination between whoever is cooking and whoever is serving becomes the pressure point.

**Service stage.** Food goes out, customers get looked after. In co-op management games, the failure mode is almost always communication, not difficulty.

**Cleanup stage.** Cleaning is listed as a distinct task, which suggests it affects the shop's condition or the next shift rather than being purely cosmetic.

Because the store page does not describe a shift timer, an economy, or a progression tree, anything beyond this is speculation. Players who want details should check the in-game tutorial and the Steam page's own screenshots and description.

## Menu and shop design: what players can reasonably expect

The two creative features are "create your own menu" and "design the shop." These are advertised, so they exist. What is not advertised is how deep either system goes.

Practical reasoning suggests menu design shapes the prep and cooking workload: more dishes usually means more prep steps and more chances for a kitchen to fall behind. A smaller menu is generally easier for a new group to coordinate. That is general kitchen logic, not a documented mechanic.

Similarly, shop design likely affects flow, but the store page does not state whether layout changes customer capacity, walk speed, or anything measurable. A player can verify this only by testing in-game.
