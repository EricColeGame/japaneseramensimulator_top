## Menus You Build Yourself — And Why That Changes the Loop

"Create your own menu" is a small phrase with large consequences. In a fixed-recipe restaurant game, the designer controls your difficulty curve; in a player-authored menu, you do. That means your menu is effectively your plan: how many dishes you commit to, how much prep each one implies, and whether your team can actually execute it during a rush.

Because the official page does not publish a recipe list, exact ingredient quantities, prices, or any code system, any specific numbers you encounter online are unverified. There is no documented recipe database from the developer as of the 14 October 2026 store data. Recipe guidance below is therefore **player practice**, not official instruction — the general logic that applies to real ramen and to kitchen-management games alike:

- Fewer dishes, done well, usually beat a sprawling menu when your team is still learning the flow.
- Broth and toppings tend to be the two timelines that matter — one is long and slow, the other is short and repetitive.
- Prep that can be done before service is prep you are not doing during service.
- If a dish creates a bottleneck at one station, it will do so every single order.

None of that guarantees success. It is a way of thinking about the menu system the developer says exists, and you should test it against your own shop.
